The bridge

Most of the naive figure is not savings.

A plain “pay the lower of the two prices on everything” formula counts unit-of-measure errors, unverified matches, one-off spot prices and gaps that exist inside a single company. Each step below removes one of these, and each dollar is attributed to the first step that removes it, so the steps sum exactly to the difference.

    The scorecard

    The matching is measured, not asserted.

    The demo data is synthetic, so the tool is scored against hidden ground truth it never reads. Tuning used three other seeds; the figures here are for a fourth, held-out seed. The same data is run through a naive design: fuzzy names and plain text similarity, with no attribute checks.

    Traceability

    Every dollar ties out to a purchasing line.

    Each headline figure has a chain back to the source rows, and re-sums from them to the cent. The workbook export carries the same figures as live formulas.

    Limits

    What this analysis cannot see.

    Stated on screen, in the deck and in the workbook, so nobody has to find out later.

    • Rebates, credits and off-invoice discounts
    • Specification, quality and lead-time differences between apparently identical items
    • Freight and delivery terms, unless incoterms are supplied
    • Contract clauses such as minimum volumes, exclusivity and change of control
    • Index-linked prices caught at different dates
    • Supplier capacity and willingness to serve the combined volume
    • Services and other spend with neither an item code nor a part number, beyond vendor and category totals
    • Corporate parent links not supplied in the parent table

    About this project

    All data is synthetic and illustrative. The two companies, their vendors, items and prices are generated; no real company or supplier is represented.

    This workflow is not new. Consulting firms and spend-analytics vendors do it with proprietary tooling. What Tie-Out offers is an open implementation in which every matching rule, every formula and every dollar can be audited. It is not legal advice, and its masked view is a presentation mode, not an access control.
